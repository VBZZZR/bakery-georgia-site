Tsvima means “rain”

About 2,500 mm of rain falls on Batumi in a year, almost five times as much as on Tbilisi. It rains on 139 days a year.

You can see why from the window. Wet air off the sea runs into the mountains of Adjara just behind the city and comes down on the boulevard, the port and our roofs.

The same rain grows the tea in Chakvi, the mandarins in the orchards and the grass on the mountain pastures where our cheese and butter come from.

So we gave the bakery the name people here most often give the weather. The oven is hot every day, and there is always a seat by the window.

What the rain grew

Tea, mandarins and the grass behind our cheese and butter all grow on rain here. Prices in lari.

If it starts to rain

Adjaruli doesn’t travel: you eat it hot, while the yolk is still runny and the cheese still stretches. That is what the long table by the window is for. While it rains over Batumi, Batumi coffee with your adjaruli is on us.

The street of ten names

We are on Memed Abashidze Street, the main street of Old Batumi since the early 20th century. With every change of power, the city gave it a new name:

  1. Rum Sokhas
  2. Nurie Sokhas
  3. Sultan Selim
  4. Mariinsky
  5. Comintern
  6. Third International
  7. Red Army
  8. Stalin
  9. October
  10. Freedom

Today it bears the name of Memed Abashidze, a Batumi-born writer and public figure (1873–1937). On the 1913 plan it is still Mariinsky Prospekt, and the seafront boulevard is Tsesarevich Boulevard. From our door it is a five-minute walk.

The street has had ten names. The weather here has only one: tsvima.

How to find us

Come in and wait out the rain. Or just come for a hot adjaruli.

  1. From the boulevard Five minutes’ walk inland from the seafront boulevard, into Old Batumi.
  2. Along the street Memed Abashidze Street, Mariinsky Prospekt on the 1913 plan. Number 23.
  3. At the door An awning in chocolate and white stripes. Leave your umbrella in the stand.
