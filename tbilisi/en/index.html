Betlemi

The street named us

Betlemi is Georgian for Bethlehem, and Bethlehem is Hebrew for “house of bread”. Our oven stands at the first step of that house.

  • 120 steps

    above our door, up to the garden of Upper Betlemi

  • 8 minutes

    how long a shoti stays on the wall of the tone

  • 1.50 ₾

    one shoti, hot, in a strip of paper

  • 8:00–20:00

    every day; we light the tone at 6:30

Our oven is in the floor

A tone is a round pit in the floor, lined with brick and clay. We light it an hour and a half before the first bread. Everything we sell has baked on its wall.

Knead

Flour, warm water, salt and yeast. For an hour and a half the dough rises under linen and grows to two and a half times its size.

Dough on the table of a Tbilisi bakery, 2018.

Pull

A 600-gram piece goes onto the lapati, a linen pad on a wooden base. The baker pulls it by the ends into the long boat of a shoti.

A baker with shoti dough at the tone, Tbilisi, 2011.

Press

The baker leans head first into the tone and presses the dough onto the hot wall. The bottom half-metre stays empty: bread would burn there.

Dough on the wall of a tone, Georgia, 2005.

Wait

Eight minutes, and the shoti is golden. A kavi hook catches it, a sapkheki scraper frees it, and it comes to you hot, in a strip of paper.

Tonis puri on the wall of a tone, 2014.

Dika is a Georgian wheat. Its Latin name, Triticum carthlicum, means “of Kartli”, the region Tbilisi stands in, and texts from the 5th century already mention it. Dika flour is scarce, so we bake dika shoti only at weekends.

We have no tables. Our only table is the staircase above the door.

How to get to the tone

Where are you walking from?

A shoti comes off the wall every eight minutes, from 8:00 to 20:00. Walking time to us: 12 minutes from Narikala, 3 from Upper Betlemi, 4 from Atashgah, 9 from Freedom Square, 10 from the Bridge of Peace, 12 from the sulphur baths.
